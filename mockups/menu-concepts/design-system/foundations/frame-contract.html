The frame contract — where a screen is allowed to stand

This supersedes the old constraint that a screen "gets roughly y 200 to y 620, full width". Full width was the problem. The valley is the reason this menu exists, and a screen that paints to both edges has spent the whole budget of the idea before it has said anything.

Every screen in 01-sumi-3d.html was moved onto this in August 2026 and measured afterwards — the numbers below are not a proposal.

THE RULE NOBODY GUESSES — SKEW IS PAINT, NOT LAYOUT

Every plate in this interface is skewX(-8deg) rotate(-1.2deg), and a bounding box on a skewed plane measures the projection, not the box. Authoring left: 160px does not put a plate's paint at x 160 — it puts it somewhere left of that, by an amount that depends on the plate's HEIGHT. This is the single most common way a screen ends up in the moat while its CSS looks correct.

So the stage's 160 and 1120 become an authored 174 and 1106 for a card, 166 and 1114 for a rail, 185 and 1095 for a full-height column. And the vertical one is the expensive one, because it scales with WIDTH: the ledger's four plates come to about 425 painted in a 384 stage, and 41 of those pixels are nothing but three wide plates rolled 1.2° rising at their ends. That is why its level bar is in the foot band and not on the stage. Budget for it before laying anything out, or a screen that fits on paper will not fit on screen.

THE OVERFLOW RULE

The stage never grows and nothing scrolls, so a set that does not fit is folded, and what leaves becomes a count on the edge it left over.

  • Lists window around the selection. The library holds thirty texts and paints six — two above, three below — with "10 ABOVE" and "14 BELOW" pinned to the stage's top and bottom edges, and a thirty-bar strip in the foot so the whole set is still on screen.
  • Chains paint at most 7 stones. The deck screen holds 76 blocks and paints three objects: the count behind you, the one open block, the one ahead. The rest is the proportional rail — which is a whole-set summary, so it takes the foot.
  • A whole-set summary may paint every member, because it is one thin object rather than a set of choosable ones. Thirty bars and 76 segments both pass.
What survives a fold, and what does not. When a card narrows, cut copy rather than type. The deck's gate line lost its tail because the card count was already in the line above it; the ascent's plinths lost their counts because the panel beside them spells those out for the selected level. What must not happen is a 9px caption becoming a 7px one — every caption in this interface is already at the floor, and the ground it stands on is a moving 3D valley.
The one deliberate deviation. The standing menu obeys the moat and the bands and does not obey the balance: it is a left column with the mountain to its right, and that composition is the reason the rest of the interface looks the way it does. Every other screen is balanced about x 640.
