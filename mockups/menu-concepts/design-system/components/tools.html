The tools

past-three.html moved the screens a session happens in onto one sheet of paper. This card is everything that was still left in the old sepia language after that: the kanji detail panel, conversation practice (the scenario tutor), and the two other things the tutor pop-up holds — chat with the tutor and image translation. None of it is built. Robbie approves this card before any of it is.

Two objects, already in the app, carry all of it. Things you consult stand on the lookup's scrim — Look it up, Every character and settings already do — so the kanji panel joins them, on Every character's own 900-wide shell. Things you do stand on the stage's sheet, the way the round, the exam and the passage do, so conversation practice gets the sheet and a place in The World. Chat and image translation are the one open question, and section EIGHT draws both answers side by side.

The numbers on these plates.

Every count, chip and limit below is one the code can produce today, and the note under each plate says where it comes from. The kanji plates use the payload the bridge actually builds for 漢 and 語 (data/dictionary_repository.py, build_kanji_detail_payload, run for this card); the conversation plates use the cafe scene's real script (lib/scenarios/cafeOrder.ts). The only invented words are the ones a model writes — the tutor's reply and the translation in section EIGHT — and those are labelled where they appear.

Backdrops. The scrim plates stand on valley-sunset.png, as settings does in past-three.html. The World has no plate of its own, so the conversation stands on valley-reading.png, the hot-spring town — the same ground assets/screen-scenes.png shows its two scenes on.

ONE — A KANJI, WITH THE DICTIONARY

Today a dark cassette modal titled Kanji details, a summary card, a readings grid, a compounds list and a stroke box, stacked in a scroller. Here it is Every character's sheet with the columns filled differently: the character, what it sounds like, what it is made of.

TWO — A KANJI, DECK ONLY

The state Robbie actually sees. The offline dictionary is not installed, so the bridge returns source: "deck_only": no meanings, no readings, no compounds, no stroke count. What ships with the app is still there — the parts, the level and theme from the deck, and the stroke-order box, whose data is bundled separately.

THREE — CONVERSATION, CHOOSING HOW MUCH HELP

Conversation practice leaves the tutor pop-up for its own screen. You arrive from The World's Scenes (level three, Scenes.tsx, built) having already picked the scene, so this screen asks the one question left: how much help.

FOUR — CONVERSATION, ASKING

The screen the scene is spent on. Today it is a chat log in a pop-up, every line a bubble, the reply box at the bottom and the hint in a floating popover. Here only the line you are answering is on the paper; the conversation behind it is one button away.

FIVE — CONVERSATION, ANSWERED

Today the correction is a line of small text inside your own chat bubble, and the next line from the barista has already arrived underneath it. Here the sheet holds the answer until you take the next line, which is the round's answered state, applied.

SIX — CONVERSATION, THE END

Today two screens — a summary that scrolls, then a separate panel of cards with four text boxes each. Here one sheet: what you met on the left, what to keep on the right.

SEVEN — THE STATES IN BETWEEN

Every one of these is a line in today's composer or a status paragraph above it. On the sheet each is the work cell's own status line and the slab's sentence; typing stays available in every one of them, which is today's rule.

EIGHT — CHAT AND IMAGE TRANSLATION: A OR B

This one is Robbie's to decide. A — fold them into Look it up, reversing that written decision: with a model running, the two dimmed routes become answerable and the tutor pop-up retires. The tutor's reply and the translation are illustrative — a model writes them.

WHAT THE LEAD SHOULD KNOW BEFORE THIS IS APPROVED

Four things on this card are new behaviour, not restyling.

The mic's CTRL+M (FOUR); the view holding the answered state until Enter while the engine has already moved on (FIVE); "Try saving again" on a failed save (SIX); paste and drop for images (EIGHT). Each is small and each is drawn because the brief's layout needs it, but none exists today.

Three numbers in the brief do not match the code, and the plates follow the code: the cafe's objectives are 1 / 4, not 1 / 5 (four required of six); the earlier-turns count is 4 on the size question (2 is the count at the order, used on FIVE); and image translation returns one English block for the whole image, not a translation per line.

One data bug surfaced while drawing: the dictionary build maps KANJIDIC's pre-2010 JLPT level straight onto N-levels (build_offline_dictionary_sqlite.py:297), so 漢 reads N3 with the dictionary and N4 without it. Not this card's to fix.
