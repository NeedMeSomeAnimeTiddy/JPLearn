LEGIBILITY OVER THE WORLD

This interface stands on a lit, moving, flat-shaded valley — not on a panel. Every legibility failure in it has been the same failure, and it has now been paid for three separate times: the figure chips, the course bars, and the calendar band. The band below is a stand-in for the ground the real thing sits on; none of this shows up against flat dark.

THE TWO INSTRUMENTS, AND WHICH TO REACH FOR

KEYLINEpaint-order: stroke fill + -webkit-text-stroke: 0.04–0.06em rgba(8,11,18,0.92). For display type over 20px. The paint order is what makes it a keyline rather than a fattened letter.
GROUNDrgba(10,9,6,0.82) + a 1px cream inset hairline + the house 2px 3px 0 rgba(0,0,0,0.45) shadow. For labels, figures, chips and every measured element.
NEVER ALPHA ON THE WHOLE THINGFading a row fades its bar and its badge with it, and the bar is the one part that still has to state a fact honestly. Dim the TYPE, not the object.
DARK ON LIGHT TAKES NEITHERInk on a cream chip needs no halo and no keyline — the chip's own drop shadow separates it from the world. A halo on 10.5px ink is the stroke width again, which is why ENTER and ESC were once the hardest words on screen.

The lesson generalised: contrast does half the work of size. 19px a row is cramped for cream over gravel and comfortable for ink on paper — which is how the printed-sheet course fits sixteen legible steps in the same rhythm a rejected design was called cramped for.
